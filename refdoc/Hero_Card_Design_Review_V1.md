\# Hero Card Design Review V1





\## Purpose



This document records the design lessons learned from Product Rule V3.1 Hero Card development.



The goal is to help future knowledge pages maintain the same design quality and avoid blindly copying visual elements.





\---



\# 1. Product Rule V3.1 as the Reference





Product Rule V3.1 is successful because it does not simply display a formula.



It creates a visual explanation of the mathematical idea.





The Hero Card communicates:



\- What are the mathematical objects?

\- How do they relate?

\- Why does the formula have this structure?





\---



\# 2. What Makes Product Rule V3.1 Successful





\## 2.1 Formula is the visual center





The formula is always the most important element.





Supporting elements exist to explain the formula.



They should never compete with the formula.





\---



\## 2.2 Visual elements have mathematical meaning





Example:





Product Rule:





f and g are not decoration.





They represent:



\- two changing functions;

\- two sources of contribution.





Every visual element should answer:



"What mathematical meaning does this represent?"







\---



\## 2.3 Colors communicate relationships





Product Rule:





Blue:



first function contribution





Red:



second function contribution





The color system helps students understand the formula.





Colors are not only for visual decoration.







\---



\## 2.4 Layout creates understanding





A successful Hero Card uses:



\- clear hierarchy;

\- balanced spacing;

\- enough whitespace;

\- focused attention.





Avoid:



\- crowded information;

\- unnecessary separators;

\- excessive labels.



\## Formula Rendering Rules





Hero Card formulas must always remain complete and readable.





Requirements:



\- The entire mathematical expression must be visible.

\- Formula containers must adapt to expression length.

\- Long formulas should reduce size or adjust layout before being clipped.

\- Mathematical structure must be preserved.





Avoid:



\- Cropped formulas.

\- Missing symbols.

\- Partial equations.

\- Breaking formulas into unreadable lines.





Priority:



1\. Complete mathematical meaning

2\. Readability

3\. Visual balance



\---



\# 3. Common Mistakes During Expansion





\## Mistake 1:



Copying Product Rule elements directly.





Wrong:



Every page uses f and g circles.





Correct:



Each topic uses its own mathematical visual language.







\---



\## Mistake 2:



Treating Hero Card as a formula container.





Wrong:



Put formula inside a box.





Correct:



Use visual design to explain why the formula exists.







\---



\## Mistake 3:



Adding decorative elements without meaning.





Avoid:



\- unnecessary lines;

\- random icons;

\- visual elements without mathematical purpose.







\---



\# 4. Topic-Specific Visual Language





Different topics should share the same design system but use different mathematical stories.





\## Product Rule



Visual idea:



Two changing functions contribute to one result.





Elements:



\- f

\- g

\- blue/red contributions







\---



\## Derivative Concept





Visual idea:



Average change approaches instantaneous change.





Elements:



\- secant line;

\- tangent line;

\- approaching behavior;

\- limit concept.







\---



\## Quotient Rule





Visual idea:



A ratio changes because numerator and denominator change.





Elements:



\- numerator;

\- denominator;

\- different contributions.







\---



\## Chain Rule





Visual idea:



Change passes through multiple layers.





Elements:



\- input;

\- inner function;

\- outer function.



\# Hero Card Content Selection Rules





\## Purpose



The Hero Card introduces the mathematical concept.



It should not display an arbitrary example formula.





\---



\## Concept Before Example





Hero Card should communicate:



"What is this concept?"





Not:



"What is one example problem?"





Avoid:



Using specific numerical examples that require later explanation.





Example:





Wrong:



\\\[

x^2+y^2=25

\\]





because students do not know why this equation appears.





Better:



Show the relationship:



\\\[

F(x,y)=0

\\]





and explain:



x and y are connected by an implicit relationship.







\---



\## Hero Card Formula Selection





The displayed formula should be:



\- Concept-defining;

\- General;

\- Immediately understandable;





Avoid:



\- Specific exercise examples;

\- Arbitrary constants;

\- Formulas only explained later in the page.



\---



\# Hero Card Concept Priority Rules





\## Purpose



The Hero Card introduces the central idea of the topic.



A mathematically correct formula is not automatically a good Hero Card.







\## Concept Before Formal Definition





Hero Card should prioritize:



1\. Core mathematical idea

2\. Relationship between concepts

3\. Intuitive understanding





Only then:



\- formal derivative definition;

\- limit definition;

\- detailed formula.







Avoid:





\- Starting with the most formal formula.

\- Displaying a formula that requires later explanation.

\- Showing incomplete or context-dependent notation.







Examples:





Bad:



Using:



\\\[

\\lim\_{\\Delta t\\to0}

\\frac{Q(t+\\Delta t)-Q(t)}{\\Delta t}

\\]



as the only Hero Card for Rates of Change.





Better:



Show:



quantity change → time change → rate of change







Bad:



Showing only:



\\\[

L(x)=f(a)+f'(a)(x-a)

\\]



without explaining:



curve → tangent line → local approximation.



\# 5. Final Design Principle





Future Hero Cards should follow:





Same product.



Same visual quality.



Different mathematical stories.





The goal is not:



"Make every page look identical."





The goal is:



"Make every page feel like part of the same learning system."
